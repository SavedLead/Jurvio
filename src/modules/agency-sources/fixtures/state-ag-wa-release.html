Win for Washington as judge blocks unlawful federal implementation of Medicaid work requirements

FOR IMMEDIATE RELEASE:
Oct 29 2026

A judge blocked the federal government’s unlawful implementation of new Medicaid work requirements that would have made it even harder for people with challenging medical conditions to get the healthcare they need, delivering a victory to Washington and a multistate coalition that brought the case in June 2026.

The U.S. District Court for the District of Massachusetts found the administration’s implementation of new Medicaid work requirements under the Big Beautiful Bill Act (H.R. 1) was unlawful. The rule adopted a new interpretation of key terms like “medically frail,” making it harder for medically vulnerable individuals to be excused from the work requirements. 

“Our state is healthier, safer, and more productive when everyone can get the care they need,” said Attorney General Nick Brown. “We will continue to defend Medicaid as the federal government uses bureaucracy and red tape to try to deny Washingtonians the care they deserve.”

Medicaid is the nation’s safety net healthcare program for low-income Americans. Congress created exemptions from Medicaid’s work requirements to ensure that people with serious illnesses and disabilities do not lose coverage or face interruptions in care. Apple Health is Washington’s Medicaid program and insures approximately 1.9 million individuals, including some of the most vulnerable people in the state, such as those experiencing homelessness or who have serious or complex medical conditions.

Despite months of working with states on implementation, CMS surprised states with the interim final rule, “Community Engagement Requirement for Certain Individuals” in June 2026. 

Past Medicaid work requirement programs have shown that rules that add red tape like the June 2026 rule cause eligible people to lose coverage, place greater strain on state Medicaid programs, safety net providers, and emergency rooms, while increasing costs as more medically frail residents become uninsured. 

In today’s decision, the District Court held that the Trump Administration’s definition of “medical frailty” is contrary to law, vacated the language, issued a permanent injunction, and granted the plaintiff states’ a six-month stay of enforcement to accommodate any errors or delays resulting from the necessary changes to implementation.

Washington’s Attorney General serves the people and the state of Washington. As the state’s largest law firm, the Attorney General’s Office provides legal representation to every state agency, board, and commission in Washington. Additionally, the Office serves the people directly by enforcing consumer protection, civil rights, and environmental protection laws. The Office also prosecutes elder abuse, Medicaid fraud, and handles sexually violent predator cases in 38 of Washington’s 39 counties. Visit www.atg.wa.gov to learn more.

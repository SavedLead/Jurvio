# Jurvio Gate 8A: Understanding, Procedure, News, Policy, and Citation Experience

**Status:** Approved  
**Date:** 2026-07-19  
**Depends on:** Approved product, data, AI, SEO and platform foundations

## 1. Experience promise

Jurvio explains litigation accurately at a fifth-grade reading level first, with expandable professional detail and source authority. Simplification must not erase procedural nuance.

Every explanation uses three layers:

1. Simple answer.
2. Important details and case-specific meaning.
3. Sources, authority and confidence.

## 2. Jurvio Understanding Layer

Subtle annotations identify legal terms, courts, procedures, roles, statutes, doctrines and cited cases.

- Desktop: hover/focus card.
- Mobile: tap-triggered bottom sheet.
- Keyboard and screen-reader accessible.
- Annotate the first meaningful occurrence per section to avoid clutter.
- Optional Plain Language Mode expands more explanations inline.
- Experienced users may reduce annotations.

Each concept stores a canonical ID, aliases, definition, implications, jurisdictional variation, sources, effective dates, review state and confidence.

## 3. Legal Procedure Intelligence

Important events answer:

- What happened?
- What does it mean?
- Why does it matter?
- What does it not mean?
- Who is affected?
- What remains unresolved?
- What usually happens next?
- Can it be challenged?

Initial procedure coverage:

- complaint, service, answer and default;
- motion to dismiss;
- discovery and discovery disputes;
- preliminary injunction and temporary restraining order;
- summary judgment;
- trial, verdict and judgment;
- dismissal with/without prejudice;
- settlement;
- appeal, stay, rehearing, en banc review and certiorari.

The case journey highlights the current stage, completed stages and plausible next stages.

## 4. Evidence-status safeguards

Every material statement is labeled where applicable:

- allegation;
- denial;
- party argument;
- evidence submitted;
- undisputed fact;
- contested fact;
- judicial finding;
- jury verdict;
- final judgment.

A party citation or assertion is never presented as court adoption.

## 5. Jurisdiction and court context

Every case may explain:

- why the court can hear the dispute;
- subject-matter and personal jurisdiction;
- federal question, diversity, removal and remand;
- venue and transfer;
- division and case-assignment rules;
- appellate path;
- binding versus persuasive authority.

Court pages show active/senior judges, vacancies, appointing president, appointing president’s party, confirmation vote, assignment divisions and appellate circuit. Jurvio does not label federal judges personally Democratic or Republican and does not imply appointment predicts an outcome.

## 6. Forum Selection Analysis

For high-interest cases, report legally available venues, stated venue basis, assignment mechanics, related-case rules, transfer/remand disputes and the number of judges who could receive the matter.

Use “judge shopping” only when attributed to a party, judge or reliable source, or when clearly labeled as a supported procedural inference. Present responses and uncertainty neutrally.

## 7. Policy Status and Next Steps

Government/public-law cases include:

- neutral policy description and legal authority;
- what changed and who is affected;
- government and challenger positions;
- current enforceability and geographic scope;
- injunction/stay/appeal status;
- available, expected and merely possible next actions;
- last-verified timestamp.

SCOTUS Path Assessment describes the remaining court path, circuit splits, national importance, finality, emergency posture and factors increasing/decreasing review likelihood. It never promises Supreme Court review.

## 8. Supreme Court, Explained

Dedicated statuses:

- petition filed;
- response requested;
- review granted/denied;
- briefing;
- oral argument;
- emergency application;
- decision;
- remand.

Explain the question presented, lower-court history, present practical effect, majority/concurrence/dissent, what changes and what happens next. A certiorari denial is not described as approval of the lower ruling.

## 9. Cases in the News

Top-level public product with:

- Trending Now;
- Most Followed;
- Major Government Cases;
- Supreme Court;
- Business Litigation;
- Civil Rights;
- Technology and AI;
- Criminal Justice/Law Enforcement when demand supports it;
- Fast-Moving Cases;
- Recently Decided;
- Upcoming Hearings.

A hot-case page answers why the case is in the news, latest verified development, current legal effect, common misunderstandings, next event and source filings.

Ranking uses filtered demand, follows, filing velocity, diverse news coverage, appellate importance and people affected. Political viewpoint is never a ranking factor. Editorial overrides require a logged reason.

Coverage links distinguish independent reporting, party statements, government statements, law-firm analysis, advocacy and academic sources. Jurvio summarizes independently and does not republish articles without rights.

## 10. Citation Context Layer

When an opinion cites another authority, its annotation explains:

- cited case/court/year;
- one-sentence holding;
- why cited here;
- proposition supported;
- binding/persuasive status;
- treatment;
- pinpoint passage;
- link to full case and relevant text.

Treatment vocabulary includes followed, applied, relied upon, quoted, distinguished, limited, questioned, criticized, declined, overruled and background mention.

The system distinguishes citations in judicial opinions from party briefs and other filings. Treatment claims require surrounding-text evidence, cited-passage evidence, confidence and correction capability.

Case pages expose authorities cited, later citing cases, treatment history and proposition-level connections.

## 11. Neutrality and prediction rules

- Jurvio explains; it does not advocate.
- Separate source facts, party positions, judicial findings and Jurvio-generated explanation.
- “Available,” “expected” and “possible” are separate statuses.
- Forecasts require evidence and confidence.
- Show uncertainty and competing procedural paths.
- No partisan labels for outcomes.
- No political commentary.
- Corrections preserve history and provenance.

## 12. AI and deterministic boundary

Deterministic systems handle citation parsing, court hierarchy, docket events, known deadlines, assignment rules and stored definitions where feasible.

AI may draft plain-language/context explanations, classify importance and propose treatment only from bounded source evidence. Outputs require structured schemas, citations, confidence, contradiction checks and risk-based review.

Approved reusable definitions are cached; Jurvio does not regenerate common legal terms per page.

## 13. SEO, AI discovery and strategic value

Create canonical pages for legal concepts, procedures, courts, policies and cited authorities. Use controlled internal linking, structured metadata, freshness, provenance and AI-readable entity representations.

The strategic asset is the connected, jurisdiction-aware explanation and reasoning graph, not the tooltip UI itself.

## 15. Acceptance principles

- Non-lawyer comprehension testing succeeds.
- Federal-litigator accuracy review succeeds.
- Every case-specific explanation links to evidence.
- Mobile and keyboard interactions work.
- Annotation density does not obstruct reading.
- Allegations never become facts through summarization.
- Prediction language reflects evidence and uncertainty.
- Neutrality and correction audits pass.

## 16. Approved decisions

1. Use the layered fifth-grade-first explanation model.
2. Treat Understanding and Legal Procedure Intelligence as core products.
3. Implement jurisdiction, court context, policy status and next-step requirements.
4. Build Supreme Court, Explained and Cases in the News as connected public products.
5. Build the Citation Context Layer with evidence-based treatment.
6. Enforce the neutrality, forecasting and evidence-status safeguards.
7. Follow the stated launch priority and phased expansion.
